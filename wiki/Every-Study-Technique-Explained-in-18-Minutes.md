# Every Study Technique Explained in 18 Minutes

**Channel:** Justin Sung (iCanStudy) | **Published:** 2026-08-02

## Summary

Dr. Justin Sung, a learning coach and former medical doctor, ranks eight popular study techniques on a tier scale (F to S). The central thesis: most popular techniques fail because they don't promote **schema building** — the formation of connected knowledge networks that enable complex problem-solving. Techniques that feel easy are usually ineffective; the best techniques demand effortful, deep cognitive engagement.

## Tier Rankings

| Technique | Tier | Reason |
|-----------|------|--------|
| Highlighting / Underlining | **F** | No active cognitive engagement; creates familiarity illusion, not real learning |
| Rereading | **F** | Marginal retention benefit; no schema building; enormous time sink |
| Blurting (Brain Dump) | **B** | Legitimate active recall, but tests isolated facts — no schema building |
| Flashcards (e.g. Anki) | **B** | Benefits from spacing effect + cued recall, but still isolated facts; drops to F/E for professionals |
| Mind Mapping | **D–S** | D if done casually; **S-tier** if executed correctly with deliberate comparative thinking |
| Feynman Technique | **A** | Forces deep processing and simplification; expertise-limited; upgrades to **S-tier** with AI feedback |
| Cornell Notes | **C** | Good starter method; most people do it incorrectly; stepping stone to mind mapping |
| Interleaving | **B–S** | B if alternating subjects; **S-tier** if used to test knowledge from multiple angles/levels within the same topic |

## Key Takeaways

- **The effort principle:** Useless techniques are popular because they are easy. Effective learning is cognitively demanding by nature.
- **Schema building is the goal:** The ability to solve complex problems, write essays, and apply knowledge depends on connected knowledge networks — not isolated facts.
- **Mind mapping (done correctly) is the top technique:** Forces externalization and organization of confused thinking. Works because the brain stores memory via connections.
- **Feynman + AI:** Teaching a concept to an AI and asking for expert-level feedback upgrades the Feynman technique to S-tier.
- **Interleaving (Sung's version):** Challenging yourself with questions at different angles and levels — not just switching subjects — is the key mechanism.
- **Flashcards are overhyped:** Valuable for rote memorization (e.g., drug dosages) but nearly useless for knowledge that requires application.
- **Learning is a cognitive skill:** Its effects are invisible and delayed, making it hard to notice when a technique is failing.
