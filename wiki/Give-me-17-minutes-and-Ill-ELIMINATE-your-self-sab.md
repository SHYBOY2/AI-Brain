# Give me 17 minutes and I'll ELIMINATE your self-sabotage forever
**Source:** [[2026-08-10-Give-me-17-minutes-and-Ill-ELIMINATE-your-self-sab.md]]
**Channel / Author:** Clark Kegley

## Summary
The concept of [[self-sabotage]] is a pattern of behavior that hinders our progress and success, often due to unconscious factors. According to Clark Kegley, recognizing and addressing [[self-sabotage]] is crucial to achieving our goals and improving our lives. By understanding the cycle of [[self-sabotage]], which involves [[triggers]], [[escape mechanisms]], [[relief]], and [[shame]], we can begin to break free from this pattern. The key to overcoming [[self-sabotage]] lies in [[self-awareness]], [[self-acceptance]], and implementing practical strategies to manage our thoughts, emotions, and behaviors.

## Key Takeaways
* [[Self-sabotage]] is a common pattern of behavior that can hinder our progress and success.
* The cycle of [[self-sabotage]] involves [[triggers]], [[escape mechanisms]], [[relief]], and [[shame]].
* Recognizing and addressing [[self-sabotage]] is crucial to achieving our goals and improving our lives.
* [[Competing commitments]] can be a major obstacle to success, where we want two things that are mutually exclusive.
* Giving ourselves permission to take action despite not feeling like it can be a powerful way to overcome [[self-sabotage]].
* Letting go of our [[addiction to control]] can help us break free from [[self-sabotage]] patterns.
* [[Environmental factors]] can contribute to [[self-sabotage]], and making changes to our environment can help us overcome it.
* [[Self-awareness]] and [[self-acceptance]] are essential for overcoming [[self-sabotage]] and achieving our goals.
* Implementing practical strategies, such as journaling and seeking support, can help us manage our thoughts, emotions, and behaviors and overcome [[self-sabotage]].

## Key Concepts
* [[Self-sabotage]]: a pattern of behavior that hinders our progress and success, often due to unconscious factors.
* [[Triggers]]: events or situations that trigger [[self-sabotage]] behaviors.
* [[Escape mechanisms]]: behaviors or activities that provide temporary relief from stress, anxiety, or other negative emotions.
* [[Relief]]: the feeling of temporary comfort or escape from negative emotions.
* [[Shame]]: the feeling of guilt, regret, or self-criticism that can follow [[self-sabotage]] behaviors.
* [[Competing commitments]]: when we want two things that are mutually exclusive, leading to [[self-sabotage]].
* [[Addiction to control]]: the need to control our environment, emotions, or outcomes, which can lead to [[self-sabotage]].
* [[Self-awareness]]: the ability to recognize and understand our thoughts, emotions, and behaviors.
* [[Self-acceptance]]: the ability to accept and love ourselves, despite our flaws and imperfections.
