# Spacing Effect

A well-established principle in learning science: reviewing material at spaced intervals (rather than in a single massed session) produces significantly stronger long-term retention.

## The Mechanism

When you learn something, allow time to pass, then review it again, the memory trace is strengthened more than if you reviewed it immediately. The gap forces the brain to work harder to retrieve the information, which deepens the encoding.

## Application: Spaced Repetition Software (SRS)

The spacing effect is most practically implemented through spaced repetition algorithms — used by apps like Anki. The algorithm tracks when each card is due for review, scheduling it at the optimal interval to maximize retention with minimal review time.

This is the primary benefit of flashcard apps over paper flashcards.

## Limitation

The spacing effect is a genuine, well-evidenced phenomenon, but it does not compensate for flashcards' fundamental weakness: **it optimizes the retrieval of isolated facts, not the building of schemas**.

> Even with perfect spacing, flashcards remain a B-tier technique because they don't force comparative or connective thinking. — [[wiki/Every-Study-Technique-Explained-in-18-Minutes]]

## When Flashcards + Spacing Work Best

- Rote memorization requirements: drug names and dosages, vocabulary, formulas
- When the knowledge unit is truly atomic and does not require relational understanding
- As a supplement to higher-order techniques like [[wiki/Feynman-Technique]] or [[wiki/Mind-Mapping]]
