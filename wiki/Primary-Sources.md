# Primary Sources

The original documents, studies, texts, or people closest to the source of an idea — as opposed to summaries, summaries of summaries, or derivative content.

## The Degradation Chain

Most online content follows a lossy compression chain:
1. Original research / primary experience
2. Book or long-form writing
3. YouTube video summarizing the book
4. Short video summarizing the YouTube video
5. TikTok / Reel summarizing the short video

By stage 5, the nuance, evidence, methodology, caveats, and context that built the original idea have been stripped away. What remains is a conclusion without a foundation — borrowed judgment rather than developed judgment.

> "By the time the idea reaches you, it's become intellectual [jelly]." — Nextcore

## Why Primary Sources Matter

1. **Evidence and methodology** — You can evaluate how strong a claim actually is
2. **Nuance and caveats** — You understand where an idea applies and where it doesn't
3. **Independent judgment** — You form your own interpretation rather than inheriting someone else's
4. **Deeper schema building** — Context allows proper integration into existing knowledge

## Practical Application

- Learning investing → read Warren Buffett's shareholder letters, not motivational clips
- Learning psychology → read the original experiments, not Instagram infographics
- Learning a craft → find the person who actually built the thing

## The Tradeoff

Primary sources are slower. They require more effort and tolerance for confusion. This is precisely why most people avoid them — and why those who don't develop a genuine competitive advantage in understanding.
