# How To ABSORB TEXTBOOKS Like A Sponge

## Summary
This page provides a step-by-step guide on how to effectively absorb information from textbooks. The approach involves a systematic method of previewing, reviewing, and reading the material to maximize retention and understanding. By following this method, readers can improve their ability to absorb and retain information from textbooks.

## Key Takeaways
* Flip through the chapter to get a sense of its length, content, and layout before reading.
* Start by reading the quiz or questions at the end of the chapter to understand what the author considers important.
* Read the bold print, such as titles, subtitles, and topic headings, to understand the structure and key points of the chapter.
* Read the first and last sentence of each paragraph to get a quick overview of the content.
* Use this approach to create a map of the material before reading the chapter in full.
* Take notes on the chapter after previewing and reviewing the material.
* This approach may seem like more work upfront, but it can save time and effort in the long run by reducing the need for re-reading and cramming.
* The method is based on the principle of repetition, which is the mother of learning.
* The approach activates the reticular activating system, or internal radar, to increase awareness and focus while reading.

## Key Concepts
* **Reticular Activating System (RAS)**: a term that refers to the brain's ability to filter and prioritize information based on what is considered important or relevant.
* **Repetition**: the process of repeating information or exposure to it multiple times to aid in learning and retention.
* **Previewing**: the act of quickly reviewing material before reading it in full to get a sense of its content and structure.
* **Reviewing**: the act of going over material again after initial exposure to reinforce understanding and retention.
* **Bold Print**: text that is highlighted or emphasized in a chapter, often including titles, subtitles, and topic headings, to draw attention to key points and concepts.
