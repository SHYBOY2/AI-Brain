# Mind Mapping

A visual thinking technique that externalizes the organization of ideas on paper, allowing the learner to see, work with, and clean up their mental model of a topic. Rated **D to S tier** depending on execution quality.

## What It Is

A mind map typically places a central topic in the middle of a page with branches radiating outward representing related concepts, sub-topics, and connections.

## Why Execution Quality Matters So Much

Mind mapping is the first technique on the study tier list that cannot be done correctly by accident. Most people apply the physical format of a mind map while continuing to think about information in the same shallow way they always have. The format does not create the benefit — the **thinking process** does.

> "Because learning happens in the brain, if you don't change the way you think about it, the technique that you do physically is not going to help you." — Justin Sung

## D-Tier Execution (Common)

- Drawing connections at random without evaluating importance or relationships
- Treating it as a visual re-presentation of notes rather than a reorganization of thinking
- Result: messy map that looks organized but reflects shallow processing

## S-Tier Execution (Correct)

The correct process involves:
1. **Externalizing confusion** — Write out everything you know, even if disorganized
2. **Evaluating importance** — Decide what is more or less central; create hierarchy
3. **Comparing ideas** — Explicitly think about how concepts relate, conflict, or combine
4. **Cleaning the structure** — Reorganize until the map reflects a coherent, accurate mental model

This is cognitively demanding. The effort is the learning. The resulting memory is deep, robust, and highly applicable.

## The Analogy

> "Much like how it's easier to clean your house with the lights on rather than with your eyes closed." — Justin Sung

Mind mapping turns on the lights: it makes tacit confusion visible so it can be resolved.

## Why Mind Mapping Beats Cornell Notes

Cornell notes can look clean even when the thinking was shallow — there is no visual feedback. A bad mind map looks messy, which signals to the learner that the thinking is still disorganized. This built-in feedback loop is one of mind mapping's key advantages.

## Personal Favorite

Justin Sung, after 13 years as a learning coach and having coached thousands of learners internationally, calls correctly-executed mind mapping his single favorite technique. He reports zero instances where proper execution failed to produce improvement.
