# Becoming Smart
## Summary
Becoming smart is often perceived as a difficult and time-consuming process, but it can be achieved through simple and effective methods. Reading and practicing what you learn are key to increasing your intelligence and becoming smarter. By applying the knowledge you gain from reading and making it useful to your brain, you can improve your problem-solving skills, learn new subjects, and sound smart in conversations.

## Key Takeaways
* The traditional education system may not be the most effective way to become smart, and alternative methods such as reading and self-study can be more efficient.
* Reading fiction can improve your reading speed and vocabulary, while reading non-fiction can provide you with interesting knowledge to share in conversations.
* To become smart, you need to read, practice what you learn, and make the information useful to your brain.
* The 20% rule applies to learning new skills, where you only need to know 20% of the knowledge to master 80% of the skill.
* Practice questions and simulations can help you learn and retain information more effectively than repetition or mind mapping.
* Textbooks are a valuable resource for learning, as they provide pure information and practice questions without distractions.
* Imagination and self-testing can help you practice and retain information, even if you don't have the opportunity to practice in real life.

## Key Concepts
* **Learning Curve**: a graph that shows how good you get at a skill over time, with the most progress made in the beginning with practice.
* **20% Rule**: the idea that you only need to know 20% of the knowledge to master 80% of a skill.
* **Practice Questions**: simulations or exercises that help you apply what you've learned and retain information.
* **Textbooks**: a format for learning that provides pure information and practice questions without distractions, ideal for those who want to learn quickly and effectively.
* **Imagination**: the ability to visualize and simulate real-life scenarios, which can help you practice and retain information.
