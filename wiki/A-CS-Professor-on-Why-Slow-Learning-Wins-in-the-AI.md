# A CS Professor on Why Slow Learning Wins in the AI Era
## Summary
Tom Yeh, a CS professor at the University of Colorado in Boulder, emphasizes the importance of slow learning in the AI era. He believes that having an answer doesn't mean you truly understand the concept, and that people can buy degrees or certificates without truly owning the knowledge. Yeh advocates for a hands-on approach to learning, where students use their hands to copy notes and understand the underlying math and algorithms. This approach helps students develop a solid foundation and focus on the core concepts that don't change, even as technology evolves.

## Key Takeaways
* Slow learning is essential in the AI era, as it allows students to develop a deep understanding of the underlying concepts and math.
* Having an answer doesn't mean you truly understand the concept, and people can buy degrees or certificates without truly owning the knowledge.
* Using your hands to copy notes and understand the underlying math and algorithms helps students develop a solid foundation.
* The core concepts in computer science, such as matrix multiplication, remain relevant even as technology evolves.
* Focusing on the surface features of technology can lead to a lack of understanding of the underlying principles.
* Acquiring skills, such as playing the piano or soccer, can help individuals develop a sense of identity and confidence that can be applied to learning new technologies.
* The willingness to learn and understand the foundation of a subject is what sets individuals apart, not just their ability to memorize equations or formulas.
* Hiring individuals who are problem solvers, team players, and have good work ethics is more important than their proficiency in AI.
* AI cannot change people, but people can change AI by developing a deep understanding of the underlying concepts and math.

## Key Concepts
* **Slow learning**: a hands-on approach to learning that emphasizes understanding the underlying math and algorithms.
* **Foundation**: the core concepts and principles that remain relevant even as technology evolves.
* **Evergreen concepts**: concepts that remain relevant and useful over time, such as matrix multiplication.
* **Surface features**: the visible aspects of technology that can change rapidly, but may not provide a deep understanding of the underlying principles.
* **Problem solving**: the ability to approach and solve complex problems, which is a key skill for individuals to develop in the AI era.
* **AI native**: individuals who are proficient in AI and can apply it to solve problems and facilitate collaboration.
