# Memorization Techniques
## Summary
Science has identified two primary methods for memorizing information: pneumonic cues and spaced repetition. Pneumonic cues involve creating associations between new information and something already familiar, such as a story or visual cue. Spaced repetition involves reviewing material at increasingly longer intervals to help solidify it in long-term memory. These techniques can be applied to a wide range of subjects, from language learning to memorizing lists of information.

## Key Takeaways
* Pneumonic cues involve creating associations between new information and something already familiar, such as a story or visual cue.
* Spaced repetition involves reviewing material at increasingly longer intervals to help solidify it in long-term memory.
* Pneumonic cues work by encoding information in the brain based on stories and points of connection, making it easier to recall.
* Spaced repetition can be implemented using index cards or other tools, with the goal of reviewing material at optimal intervals for retention.
* The key to effective spaced repetition is to review material at increasingly longer intervals, rather than simply repeating it at fixed intervals.
* Pneumonic cues and spaced repetition can be used in combination to create a powerful memorization system.
* These techniques can be applied to a wide range of subjects, from language learning to memorizing lists of information.
* The human brain remembers information based on stories and points of connection, making pneumonic cues a effective way to encode new information.
* Spaced repetition is an efficient way to review material and help it stick in long-term memory.

## Key Concepts
* **Pneumonic cues**: associations between new information and something already familiar, such as a story or visual cue.
* **Spaced repetition**: reviewing material at increasingly longer intervals to help solidify it in long-term memory.
* **Encoding**: the process of creating associations between new information and something already familiar, making it easier to recall.
* **Retention**: the ability to recall information over time, which can be improved through spaced repetition and pneumonic cues.
* **Index cards**: a tool that can be used to implement spaced repetition, with the goal of reviewing material at optimal intervals for retention.
