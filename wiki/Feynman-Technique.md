# Feynman Technique

A learning method named after physicist Richard Feynman, based on the principle that if you cannot explain something simply, you do not understand it.

## Core Method

1. Study a concept
2. Explain it as if teaching it to a complete beginner (a child, a non-expert)
3. Identify where your explanation breaks down or becomes vague
4. Return to the source and fill the gaps
5. Repeat until the explanation is simple, accurate, and complete

## Why It Works

The act of teaching forces the brain to:
- Reconstruct the full logical narrative of the concept
- Prioritize what is most important vs. peripheral
- Simplify jargon into underlying meaning
- Expose every gap in understanding immediately

This is a high-order form of [[wiki/Active-Recall]] that also supports [[wiki/Schema-Building]] because it requires understanding how ideas fit together, not just isolated facts.

## Tier Rating: A → S (AI-Assisted)

Per [[wiki/Every-Study-Technique-Explained-in-18-Minutes]] (Justin Sung):
- **A tier** as a baseline: better than flashcards and blurting on every dimension
- **Expertise limitation:** If you're a beginner teaching a beginner, you may not know if your explanation is accurate — you can't give yourself good feedback
- **S tier with AI assistance:** Teach the concept to an AI and ask it to evaluate you at the level of an expert. Eliminates the expertise limitation.

## AI-Assisted Variant (S-Tier)

Teach a concept verbally or in writing to an AI → ask the AI to challenge your explanation, find gaps, and evaluate accuracy at an expert level. This upgrades the technique from A to S tier.

Practical use: explaining while driving (dictate to phone → AI evaluates).

## Connection to Self-Education

In [[wiki/The-Art-of-Becoming-Dangerously-Self-Educated]], the same principle appears as "Learning in Public": the moment you try to explain an idea, your brain finds every hole in your understanding. A blank page is a better teacher than another book.

This is the Feynman technique applied to creation: writing, building, and publishing force the same gap-exposure mechanism.
